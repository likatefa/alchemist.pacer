THE FRAMEWORK

What actually gets marked on the chart

STEP 01 — BIAS

The storyline

Monthly and weekly structure sets the narrative: is price building higher, lower, or stuck in a range? Daily then becomes the retracement that either confirms or challenges that bias before any entry is considered.

STEP 02 — LEVELS

Support & resistance, from open and close

Levels are drawn from candle bodies, not wicks — a bullish candle into a bearish one marks resistance, the reverse marks support. Once broken, a level flips role: resistance that fails becomes support, support that fails becomes resistance, feeding the next leg of the trend.

STEP 03 — CONFLUENCE

Fibonacci as a second opinion

A fresh, untested level that lines up with a key Fibonacci retracement is treated as a stronger reaction zone than either tool would suggest on its own — two independent readings agreeing on the same price.

STEP 04 — TIMING

Dropping to the entry timeframe

Once the higher timeframe confirms a level, the trade is timed on 4H, 1H, or 15-minute candles — watching for the reversal patterns and range breaks (structure shifts, gap fills, liquidity sweeps) that signal the level is actually holding.

08:00–10:00 LONDON

The killzone. Peak liquidity, where false breakouts and genuine reversals both live — traded only after Asian session highs or lows are swept.

13:00–15:00 NY

The candle range set at the New York open is used to read direction, then retested on the lower timeframe for a precision entry.

Not a shortcut. A discipline — built for the account that can't afford to guess twice.
